## Concrete regional composition and expansion plan

**Size is not accepted yet.** Provisionally reserve roughly 140–160m authored envelopes for Verge/Fen, 120–140m for Emberfall, an elongated 120×160m Windscar, and around 140m for Heartwood. These are planning envelopes with substantial scenic/expansion reserve, not instructions to multiply decoration or make every meter traversable immediately. Keep the next useful discovery about 15–25 seconds of ordinary travel away and offer an extraction decision within the existing 5–10 minute expedition target. Measure with the slowed Explorer. Add future pockets behind believable overgrowth, collapsed crossings or distant terraces, not blank reachable acreage.

For the edge repair, **separate the visual ground extent from reachable bounds**. Extend a low-cost visual skirt approximately 25–35m beyond the collision enclosure, with horizon masses beyond it where visible. Place a continuous, irregular collision barrier *inside* the raw edge: overlapping cliff slabs, dense root walls, fallen massive growths or ruined embankments with visually justified gaps. Start with 4–7m-high masses and inspect from the actual 5m-high camera; height alone is not a guarantee. Layer low foreground blockers, taller masses and cheap distant silhouettes. Retain invisible bounds only behind that composition. Keep at least 3m clear around gate/waypoint arrival volumes and preserve all authored routes. Author and export must preserve the scenic skirt and blockers, and inactive regions must remain isolated.

No expansion should be called complete until walking to a near-edge resource/ruin and rotating through four quadrants shows continuous landscape, no ruler-straight cut, no visible invisible-wall stop, and no missing collision behind a visually blocking cliff. Include elevated shoulder/perch views, where a ground-level screen can falsely pass.

## Small living-world systems, in useful order

1. **Perception before population.** Give the first feeding glade a safe rear/side sneak approach, a readable frontal sightline and visible cover on one flank. Sneaking should avoid notice except in front view or very close, following the latest owner steering. Use short directional vision with existing Rapier line-of-sight queries, smaller close-range hearing when sneaking, larger noise from running/attacking, and a brief investigate state. A clear head turn/attention cue should precede aggression. Sparse grass should not secretly count as solid cover.
2. **One bounded group per test habitat.** Store 3–5 validated candidate positions in a single habitat; select a pair or trio on region entry/respawn. Reject blocked ground, water-incompatible positions, recent hazards and overlapping actor footprints. Keep a minimum separation and bounded retry count. Persist/reuse the existing creature ownership rather than creating a second spawning manager.
3. **Different social reactions.** Two Mosslings can flee toward a validated refuge when startled. A Thornprowler pair at the optional hollow can share a short local alert when one is struck; signal the second actor before it joins. Cap each encounter's simultaneous attackers; no whole-region alarm chains. Keep these encounters out of mandatory first-harvest and trap-release spaces.
4. **Ambient flight and readable danger.** Add a few non-colliding alien bugs near water/flowers with a bounded local animation; this is atmosphere, not another combat system. Add one clearly exposed pit/ravine as an optional detour, with a visible lip, depth cue, safe bypass and a recoverable early failure. Keep the current thorn-course hazard as its own explicit traversal contract.

Use the existing Three.js/Rapier/CreatureSystem for these small needs; no new navigation engine, flocking framework or behavior-tree package is justified by this pass. Suitable primary references informed the recommendations: [Jane Ng, Making the World of Firewatch](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Ng_Jane_MakingTheWorld.pdf), especially representative world art plus actual traversal for judging distance and discovery (pp.54–76); [Brendon Chung, Wayfinding & Storytelling Techniques](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022117/level-design-in-a-day), for architecture communicating navigation; [Guerrilla, The AI of Horizon Zero Dawn](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/the-ai-of-horizon-zero-dawn), documenting group coordination and differently sized agents. The small habitat/alert implementation above is this review's recommendation, not a claim to reproduce Horizon's architecture.
