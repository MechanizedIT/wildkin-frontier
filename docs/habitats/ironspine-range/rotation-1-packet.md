## Structural direction after target selection

1. Keep a clear central walking lane in each room while making the gully’s sidewalls, the ramp’s shoulder, and the crown’s exposed lip legible in portrait.
2. Shape one continuous local relief story rather than stamping a broad rectangular terrain field: gully floor → alternating shallow banks/ramp → shelf lip. Preserve source/resource/home footprints and full movement disks before any local profile blend.
3. Group existing low scenery as distinct gully edge, ramp edge, and crown edge clusters. Suppress/reposition only bounded cosmetic records after proving source/route/collision parity; do not raise shared caps or scatter decoration across the allocation.
4. Make the existing lower and crown iron readable as two stops without relocating their identities or changing depletion, reward, save, wildlife, or collision contracts.
5. Treat an asset gap as a separate, reviewed lane only after native kit renders and full-footprint/projection evidence show that current admitted stones cannot form the selected target’s ridge silhouette. No asset is authorized by this packet.

## Hard preservation and review gates

- Preserve all current resource IDs, transforms, harvest/depletion behavior, wildlife homes and full roam/leash disks, scenery outside the bounded selected rooms, alternate-world neutrality, Camp, and other habitats.
- Avoid recursive terrain/ecology sampling. Use existing terrain, ecology, wildlife, and scenery owners; no new save schema or headline system.
- Before source implementation: root captures the four baseline views and an ordinary-input route; a target author produces two to four portrait directions; an independent director selects one and checks camera/asset feasibility; then a concrete brief maps it to bounded terrain/ecology changes.
- The visit has one structural pass and up to two focused repairs. Independent review judges each material pass from matching actual captures. A repeated failure closes the visit as HOLD and advances the rotation.
