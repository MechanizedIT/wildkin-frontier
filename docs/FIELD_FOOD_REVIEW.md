# Trail ration — September 11 candidate

A placed Salvage bench can now make a Trail ration from two secured Berries and one Fiber. Assign it through Pack to an existing quick slot. During an expedition, Eat restores one health and consumes one ration. Selecting it, holding Use, opening Pack or attempting to eat at full health does not repeatedly spend food. Exact cost and recovery remain provisional balance choices.

The existing field-supply transaction owns the persistent stack, and player combat owns health. Food checks the active expedition and living/injured player before saving consumption; a failed save neither consumes the item nor heals. Catalog-driven load, export/import and quick-slot persistence already support the item, so no parallel inventory or new save schema was introduced. Medkits and taming supplies keep their existing action paths.

Independent production proof used an isolated 844×390 browser, disclosed Camp materials/station and injury fixtures, then native controls. It crafted three rations through the physical station cycle, assigned slot four without spending, held Use to recover from three to four health once, opened Pack while holding without repeat, ate once more to reach five health, and kept the final ration on a full-health attempt. Reload retained the selected slot and remaining ration. All 31 focused food/equipment/station tests pass; native runs report zero page errors. Evidence: `.dream-loop/overnight-trail-ration/README.md`, its receipts and nine actual images. Root independently viewed the recipe picker and injured/equipped frame.

The existing berry icon and berry-on-leaf station product are reused provisionally. They currently resemble the lure; this is functional food production, not a new accepted food-art asset or eating animation. Actual earned-material pacing and physical-phone comfort remain for playtest.

The preceding bed audit found that normal Camp return and expedition start already restore full health. No redundant player-sleep feature was added. The Wildkin bed remains decorative pending its own companion-rest/art work.
