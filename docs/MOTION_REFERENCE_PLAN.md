## Video, pose boards and actual animation data

A continuous side-view reference is useful for choosing a readable movement target: cadence, contact order, push-off, chest/pelvis loading, head lag and transition timing. A short side pose board extracted from that same sequence helps diagnose specific phases. A generated sheet of unrelated poses is weaker because anatomy and camera can change between images. A rendered “3D sprite sheet” is still flat pictures and contains no skeleton, depth, weights or editable animation curves.

Generated video can invent slipping paws, changing limbs and unsupported weight shifts. Treat it as a proposal for appearance/motion, and inspect it before fitting the real rig. A real animated quadruped donor is preferable when its movement suits the target: it supplies consistent three-dimensional joint motion and can be inspected from multiple sides. Real animal video can supplement it for weight and timing; do not confuse observational reference with rights to redistribute the source footage.

[Puppeteer](https://github.com/Seed3D/Puppeteer) is an official research example of rigging a mesh and fitting animation with video guidance. This is a separate processing pipeline, not evidence that image/video generation directly exports a game rig. The Twitter/YouTube demo described by Chris was not identified. Neither that exact Astra workflow nor a reliable local video-to-motion capability has been verified. No research pipeline was installed or run.

## Mesh2Motion: concrete compatibility findings

Official [product](https://mesh2motion.org/) and [asset repository](https://github.com/Mesh2Motion/mesh2motion-assets) provide fox rigs, hand-keyed motion, editable Blender sources and GLB export. The application is free, browser-based and MIT licensed; supplied art/rigs/animation are CC0. The source trees inspected were pinned to app commit `2d3d1ff03247d9e7e830d1ae375653da4e2146e2` and art commit `9ba82162522213f3226c099ce6d0576179556a35`.

Downloaded evidence is isolated in `.dream-loop/overnight2-motion-reference/`: 979,565 bytes of public source/license files, two fox GLBs, one skeleton-reference PNG, and Walk/Run preview MP4s. `download-provenance.json` records exact URLs, byte counts and SHA256 hashes. The MP4s were retained but **not played or visually admitted** in this audit. The skeleton PNG was inspected; it shows a long-backed, long-tailed canine stance with articulated shoulders and lower paws. No complete Blender source pack was downloaded.

The donor animation GLB is 788,588 bytes, SHA256 `80e80641a5692a14aba7616a46d2a28d3595479764887c578c766b99dba8a0d0`. It has 49 skin joints and 14 clips: Bark, Bite, Death, Fall, Fetch, Howl, Idle, Idle Alert, Jump, Rest Pose, Run, Sit, Sneak, Walk. All raw clips begin at 1/30 s; Walk ends at 1 s and Run at .466667 s. Thus their sampled spans are .966667 s and .433333 s, respectively, not established physical travel speeds. These raw timestamps require normalization/rechecking if adapted. There is no Hurt clip; Bite is only a possible Attack reference and would need target-specific readability/timing review.

| Contract | Mesh2Motion fox | Current Mossling V3 | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Body | root → Hips → Spine_1 → Spine_2 → Spine_2.001 → Spine_3 → Spine_4 → Head → Nose | pelvis → spine → chest → neck → head | No one-to-one torso transfer; several source rotations must be composed or redistributed deliberately. |
| Forelimb, each side | Shoulder → Upper → Lower → Ankle → Foot → Tip | front_upper → front_lower → front_paw | Shoulder and distal motion require fitting; matching a few names loses joint contributions. |
| Hindlimb, each side | Pelvis → Upper → Lower → Ankle → Foot → Foot_1 → Tip | rear_thigh → rear_shin → rear_metatarsal → rear_paw | The chain has different support and articulation; preserve Mossling's stifle/hock/paw interpretation. |
| Secondary motion | Several tail, ear, chin and stomach joints | Three tail joints and flower_back, with semantic head/flower attachments | No fox counterpart for flower_back; retain fitted attachment behavior. |
| Neutral transforms | Fox-specific local bone bases and segment lengths | Fitted to compact, asymmetric Mossling geometry | Bone-name mapping cannot correct local joint axes, proportions or paw support. |

Static GLB JSON supports the neutral mismatch independently of the reference image. For example, the left foreleg lower-joint offset is approximately .410 donor units versus .209 for Mossling, and its local quaternion is approximately `[-.186, 0, 0, .983]` versus `[0, -.008, -.328, .945]`. Hips/pelvis parents and local orientations also differ. These are raw local values, not world-space dimension or visual-quality measurements. `glb-structure-audit.json` retains complete joints, transforms and clip ranges.

The pre-rigged workflow does exist: choose a source skeleton, load a target skinned model, map target bones to source bones, preview selected clips, then export. However, [the pinned retarget service](https://github.com/Mesh2Motion/mesh2motion-app/blob/2d3d1ff03247d9e7e830d1ae375653da4e2146e2/src/retarget/AnimationRetargetService.ts) applies its advanced rest-pose swing/twist solver only to Human sources. The nonhuman path renames mapped tracks and copies their values; unmapped source tracks are skipped. It does not solve this fox-to-Mossling rest-basis or chain mismatch. Automatic name matching is a convenience, not anatomical validation.

Important correction to a naive raw-GLB diagnosis: [AnimationUtility](https://github.com/Mesh2Motion/mesh2motion-app/blob/2d3d1ff03247d9e7e830d1ae375653da4e2146e2/src/lib/processes/animations-listing/AnimationUtility.ts) cleans most translation/scale tracks, retaining rotation and the configured position-tracking bone; explicitly marked root-motion clips have separate treatment. Do not claim the normal app blindly copies every limb translation. Retained hips movement still needs scale/support calibration, and directly copied local quaternions remain unsuitable for this different rig without fitting. Import name sanitation also means raw `.L`/`.R` names must be checked in the loaded mapping, not assumed unchanged.

The existing Mesh2Motion skeleton-fitting workflow could make its own template compatible with a newly weighted mesh. Applying that to Mossling would reopen neutral anatomy, weights, cheek/foreleg leakage and flower attachment gates. That cost is not justified by this audit. Donor landmark/contact measurements on the existing fitted Mossling rig are the smaller experiment if a later comparison establishes a benefit.

## Bounded next test and review sequence

1. **Select references from an observed defect.** First play the already-retained current-V3 native clips continuously at normal speed. Identify a concrete issue, such as weak forepaw loading during fast travel. Keep Wolf as baseline; compare the small Mesh2Motion Walk/Run previews only if that issue remains. Select a generated/real side-view target only if it communicates the desired correction better. Record source, permitted use and intended gait; no new locomotion states are implied.
2. **Prepare matching continuous clips and a side pose board.** Use at least three complete walk/run cycles at normal speed, a fixed side camera and a visible ground reference. Supplement with rear/three-quarter views and 8–12 labelled phases. Preserve timing and left/right identity. Reject anatomy drift, cuts or contact ambiguity. Stills supplement continuous inspection and cannot replace it.
3. **Fit only after the reference wins.** Preserve the actual Mossling mesh, UVs, fitted 23-joint rig and semantic attachments. Translate donor movement into normalized landmark/contact trajectories, then solve against Mossling's own neutral bases and fixed limb lengths. Distribute torso motion, calibrate pelvis movement and raw travel distance, retain in-place horizontal clips, and keep the five existing action names. Verify loaded neutral/loaded/gathered poses before a complete bake. This step is a future candidate study, not performed work.
4. **Review the exact export independently.** Rehash the candidate; check zero-start ranges, loop continuity, sole slip/penetration, support, reach and attachments. A reviewer who did not author the fit must play normal-speed complete clips. A video saved but not actually played remains unreviewed. Reject a fit that merely passes numerical contacts while looking weightless.
5. **Confirm native behavior and phone acceptance.** Start at Camp with a bonded Mossling selected, then move into open ground toward Verdant Verge. Walk away, accelerate until it catches up, stop, and curve around a visible solid obstacle. Correct behavior is readable push-off and loaded footfalls, smooth Idle/Walk/Run transitions, grounded stopping and stable head/flowers. Sliding, hovering, repeated snapping or stretched flowers fail. In Verdant Verge, approach a wild Mossling and watch its walk-to-flee transition; check both visible size variants. Safely trigger its combat action and damage response in the existing test fixture; the bite/attack and flinch must be recognizable at normal gameplay distance. Label any diagnostic fixture instead of claiming earned progression. Check companion/wild motion, combat, Author preview/static placement and packaged loading after any integration. Chris's physical-phone observation remains distinct from desktop or emulated viewport proof.

Do not start a replacement rig or a new local inference installation to complete this research task. The immediate recommendation is continuous current-V3 review, followed only by the smallest defect-led comparison.
