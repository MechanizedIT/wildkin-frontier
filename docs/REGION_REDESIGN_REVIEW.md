# Region redesign — independent level review and first-slice brief

September12,2026. Reviewer: `camp_native_review`. Planning recommendation, not implemented world design or owner approval of exact dimensions. Chris's new direction is authoritative: substantially larger regions, more discovery, much more actual verticality, and ordinary jumping instead of pads/checkpoint courses. Rootfall V2's independent model PASS8.0 remains separate.

## Evidence and diagnosis

Inspected the actual full planning PNGs for starter Camp, expanded Camp and all five regions in `.dream-loop/visual-atlas/2026-09-12/maps/`. Read the atlas manifest/README, current scope, GAME_DESIGN, beta/story proposals, current world surface data, terrain renderer, Rapier terrain registration and natural boundary source. No game/world edits, generated images, browser or Blender run.

The atlas is an authored-spawn planning snapshot, not live enemy positions or player discovery state. Its source hash is `6b038aee3550fa8ed29a458629fee2ed6f0dce425ff121b1383ceddec2ce46ec`; current world read during this review hashes `ddc007891292f61b9aac618f68a3c7d2db72561bdd79375a57b624d7dcbde4d3`. Current source includes Rootfall wiring/placement changes absent from that atlas. Treat the images as the recorded layout baseline, not fresh native proof of Rootfall.

Chris's criticism is supported. The same course appears beside a broad central route in every expedition region. Path branches look different on a diagram, but generally cross open ground with little terrain forcing a meaningful route choice. Later maps are already larger yet contain large lightly dressed fields. Increasing those rectangles and distributing more props would increase walking without proportionate discovery.

The perimeter has substantial apparent height, but that height is largely inaccessible scenery. The playable interior is mostly a low surface with isolated oval swells. High points seldom reveal another route, conceal a reward below them or change an encounter. Verticality must become places the player occupies at different heights, connected by understandable climbs, descents and occasional ordinary jumps.

## Distinct region briefs

**Camp — a defended home in a larger landscape.** Both Camp maps show a compact useful apron and a large mostly empty perimeter tile. Keep the100m building convention and the newly admitted expansion. Concentrate future use in adjoining clearings with direct service access, a storage terrace and a sheltered Wildkin pocket. A short ridge lookout can show the crash trail without putting repeated jumping between the pod and crafting. Do not enlarge Camp simply because expedition regions expand; keep saved construction and clear divider openings intact.

**Verdant — wooded escarpment and first salvage trail.** The current Survey wreck is the best grounded narrative destination; the central route, quiet west shore and eastern ore patch need terrain relationships. Make the west lowland a sheltered Mossling/berry habitat, the east a connected stepped escarpment, and the north the narrow Rootfall exit. A high shelf reveals a small previously hidden supply find and looks back toward the wreck. Provide a broad ramp loop and one optional short natural jump shortcut. Trees cluster into recognizable groves with gaps for observation and harvesting; scenic roots/foliage do not imitate every harvestable silhouette. This is the first redesign.

**Shatterfen — basin, dry rims and interrupted sightlines.** Two simple oval pools and a central zigzag currently dominate. Build an asymmetric connected low basin with raised dry banks, sheltered Tidefin feeding shore and a4–9m observatory rise. One route follows a safe long bank; another crosses a few broad low stone shelves and short gaps, then returns by a dry ramp. The receiver/wreck fragments explain why the explorer climbs the rim. Keep the Tidefin snare area on a generous dry shore and its view clear. No swimming, underwater route or elevated water system is assumed. Reuse the current water height until a deliberately scoped change supports more.

**Emberfall — terraced works and territorial crossing.** The current northwest ruin cluster is promising but sits beside a largely empty warm field. Make one broken industrial/alien precinct occupy linked terraces with a low service yard, a higher ore cut and an upper forge overlook. Separate manufactured crash salvage from older alien masonry. Let Emberhorn occupy a wide lower lane suitable for seeing and evading its committed charge; a quieter contour route gives an avoidance alternative. Short collapsed steps can hide side supplies, while the main ascent is walkable. Avoid turning every ruin roof into an identical jumping strip.

**Windscar — exposed ascent with sheltered rest shelves.** A map called Cliffs currently has only2.5m interior relief and a long painted approach. Its defining route should ascend a winding escarpment through three broad shelves, with visible destinations above and previous ground below. Place the quiet Skydancer chime perches on sheltered benches, not narrow mandatory precision platforms. Offer one short gap crossing versus a longer contour detour; each landing must be visible from the native approach. Rock ribs and changing sightlines make the climb feel large. Ordinary jump does not authorize gliding, grappling or lethal-fall platforming.

**Heartwood — an enclosing root amphitheatre.** The current core route is a loop painted across an open field; the Guardian's spatial identity is weak. Build an entry shelf that reveals the central sanctuary, two descending approaches and a generous combat floor enclosed by large rooted terraces. The low route is direct; the outer path exposes a supply alcove and a view of the Core's protected setting. Keep combat telegraphs and lateral escape space readable. Use a safe walkable Core approach; the finale should test expedition preparation and Guardian combat, not repeat the same optional course. Preserve recoverable Core and extraction ownership.

Across all five, natural boundaries should follow a few convincing cliffs, dense rooted banks, water edges and narrow mouths. The current continuous rectangular escarpment can remain a distant failsafe, but its outline should not be the only landform. Do not hide an invisible wall in a visibly inviting meadow. Do not add hundreds of rocks to fill unused area; first give each accessible pocket a purpose and each major ridge a navigational role.

## Engineering and proof constraints

The shared terrain currently takes the maximum of smooth elliptical hills, subtracts shallow water and blends constant-elevation route strips. It is a single surface height at eachX/Z. Hills and graded routes are validated only up to5m. Simply overlapping hills will not add their heights, and long constant-elevation strips will not create a gradual climb. A6–8m first region requires a small explicit shared data/validation change across Author, import/export, renderer, ground query and collision. Higher later ranges are not permission for an unbounded terrain framework. First use several deliberate landforms and broad transitions; no tunnels, stacked walkable overhangs or bridges are required.

The actual terrain mesh samples at approximately0.7m and the same buffers become a Rapier trimesh. Small jump edges need deliberate collision geometry and enough resolution to match visible lips. The proposed140×90m base is about51,600 terrain triangles versus26,450 for80×80 before boundary/props; larger area has real cost. Keep foliage/prop population bounded, reuse admitted assets and measure native loading/frame cost before applying expansion to every region. Water visuals currently use one fixed low plane; do not put an unsupported elevated lake in the first target.

Every meaningful region review should include the standard clean overhead and anchor overlay, with scale/north/source hash and relevant state. **Overhead is insufficient to approve verticality.** Require the same locked native-pitch camera positions in baseline and candidate at arrival, bottom of climb, mid-shelf, upper overlook, descent and Rootfall approach; also require a side/elevation view with ground, shelf heights, ramp profiles, capsule and measured jump arc. A jump demonstration from a diagnostic position proves only that jump, not the whole route.

The first playable proof is an ordinary arrival→Survey→upland ascent→optional find→distinct descent→Rootfall approach→safe return loop, including at least one failed optional jump with understandable recovery. Verify visible harvest targets and calm wildlife observation on both low/high ground, correct shadows/occlusion under the accepted camera, blocked versus repaired throat, save/reload and supported waypoint/return spawns. Preserve the actual touch jump/camera ownership from the separate jump slice. Independent level/art judgment must assess discoveries and route readability from native views, with overhead as the planning companion; aggregate checks alone cannot establish a richer region.
