# Sunscar regional province — R3 visual verdict

**HOLD — 5.5/10**

| Criterion | Score | Finding |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Composition | 2.0/3 | The revised phase produces a more useful broad basin between offset pale ribs, and the overhead no longer repeats R2's same centered band placement. The forms remain oversized and largely parallel, with long straight stepped edges; they lack the target's pinched, broken ridge rhythm and varied basin widths. |
| Lighting | 1.0/3 | Player, props, and ground remain legible, but the ribs still have little slope-facing highlight or sustained side shadow. Even in the overview, most separation comes from the palette boundary rather than modeled relief. |
| Materials | 1.5/3 | Pale sand and dusty mauve remain coherent and distinct from the lush province. Their soft, nearly identical mottling keeps the surface visually flat compared with the target's granular breakup, layered slope values, and stronger rock-versus-sand response. |
| Details | 1.0/1 | Existing rocks, plants, and turquoise minerals now form clearer toe and basin-pocket groups with deliberate open ground between them. They support the new overhead composition, although most become too small or fall below the controls at portrait scale. |

The fixed portrait at x -640, z -335, looking north (-Z) with a 42° pitch is only a slight improvement over R2. A faint diagonal band enters the upper playfield and the nearby pocket is more consolidated, but there is still no readable crest, flank, basin wall, or midground destination. The player and controls remain clear; the terrain itself still reads as a flat beige/mauve plane. This fails the required ordinary-play terrain-readability gate.

Of R1, R2, and R3, **R3 is the preferred visual candidate**. R1 was dominated by one swollen stripe; R2 established multiple ribs but repeated broad centered bands and left the portrait view featureless; R3 has the strongest overhead phase, basin spacing, and contour-led asset grouping. That preference is provisional visual selection after the capped three rounds, not an 8/10 art admission.

The remaining debt is structural: the terrain field needs genuinely narrower, broken and asymmetric ribs with enough vertical relief and normal/slope contrast to survive the portrait camera; the material treatment needs slope-dependent values and finer granular breakup; and pocket placement needs to remain visible outside the joystick and action-control footprint. Another pass using the same broad smooth bands, soft shared material response, and sparse existing prop scale is unlikely to close that gap. Preserve R3 as the strongest usable candidate and move on rather than starting a fourth pass of the same approach.

These stills do not establish traversal, collision, continuous movement, native physics behavior, or physical-phone performance. Root must validate native physics and traversal separately before treating the selected candidate as playable terrain.
