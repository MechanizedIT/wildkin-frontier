# Sunscar regional province — R2 visual verdict

**HOLD — 5.0/10**

| Criterion | Score | Finding |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Composition | 2.0/3 | The same 144 m overhead now resolves into multiple pale ribs around mauve basins, a clear improvement over R1's single 164 m stripe. The 92 m bands are still broad, nearly parallel, and continuous across the frame; their stepped edges read as a sampled mask rather than broken natural ridges. |
| Lighting | 1.0/3 | Soft shadows keep actors and props legible, but the landforms receive little slope-facing highlight or broad side shadow. The overhead boundaries read mainly through color, and the normal portrait view does not reveal a lit rib flank or basin wall. |
| Materials | 1.5/3 | Pale sand and dusty mauve remain a coherent Sunscar palette. Both surfaces are uniformly mottled and similar in response, well short of the target's granular breakup and stronger separation between raised sand and recessed basin ground. |
| Details | 0.5/1 | Existing rocks, plants, and turquoise mineral forms provide scale, with somewhat better grouping near the central basin. They remain sparse and visually detached from the rib contours, especially at normal play scale. |

R2 is more regionally distinct in the overhead, but it does not pass the ordinary portrait gate. Looking north from the fixed camera, the player stands on a largely featureless mauve plane; no rib silhouette, flank, or basin edge creates a midground destination. The player and HUD remain readable. These stills do not establish traversal, collision, movement feel, or performance.

For the final pass, make two changes:

1. Rework the nearest rib so a raised, side-lit flank is unmistakable from the fixed portrait view, while pinching and offsetting its overhead edges to remove the long stepped parallel bands.
2. Consolidate the existing rocks, dry plants, and mineral accents into a few toe and basin-pocket clusters that trace the landform, leaving deliberate open sand between them.

Use the locked target and existing assets; the remaining gap is landform readability and contour-led composition, not a need for a new target or new art family.
