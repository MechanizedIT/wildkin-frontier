# Sunscar regional province — R1 visual verdict

**HOLD — 4.5/10**

| Criterion | Score | Finding |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Composition | 1.5/3 | The same-camera overhead now has a clear diagonal Sunscar band and is substantially distinct from the green baseline. It reads as one swollen stripe, while the target is organized by several narrower, offset ribs and basins with a stronger directional rhythm. |
| Lighting | 1.0/3 | The warm value shift is readable, but the terrain remains softly and evenly lit. The target uses slope-facing highlights and broad shadowed sides to make the ridges feel raised; R1 often reads as color painted across nearly flat ground. |
| Materials | 1.5/3 | Pale sand and dusty mauve establish the right regional family. Surface response is still uniform and softly mottled compared with the target's layered, granular rock-and-sand breakup and clearer slope-dependent color. |
| Details | 0.5/1 | Sparse rocks, turquoise mineral forms, and dry plants give some scale. They are tiny and scattered in the overhead, without the target's deliberate rock clusters, debris trails, and basin ecology pockets. |

The portrait view is clean and immediately different from the lush province. Its open foreground and restrained palette keep the player, controls, and nearby harvestable readable, and the visible grade looks approachable from this still. It is also too empty at normal play scale: the ridge is mostly a distant color boundary, so there is little midground structure to invite movement or communicate the province's ribbed geography. A still cannot establish actual traversal, collision, movement feel, or native performance.

For R2, make the largest change in the height-field silhouette: split the single broad diagonal band into two or three broken, asymmetric parallel ribs separated by darker shallow basins, with enough slope lighting/value contrast that the forms remain obvious in the portrait view. Then cluster existing rocks and sparse ecology along rib toes and basin pockets to reinforce those contours. This would close more of the target gap than adding more uniformly scattered props or only increasing color saturation.
