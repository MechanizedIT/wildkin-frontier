# Skybreak ecology R1 — independent visual verdict

**HOLD — 5.1/10** (PASS requires at least 8.0)

- **Composition — 1.2/3.** The inherited mountain and supported camera remain coherent, and R1 adds a few new points of interest, but the ecology is still distributed as isolated specks. In the overview, neither lowland approach forms a useful supply-thicket landmark and the crown lacks the target's crystal-and-flower anchor. The overhead view shows additions, but their spacing and scale do not create a readable route. East-return stones do not establish an intermittent repeated rhythm. In the portrait crown view, the Mossling finally enters the lower edge, but it appears on bare ground without a habitat grouping, visible cap flowers, or a crystal discovery.
- **Lighting — 2.2/3.** Exposure and the soft mint/sand palette are clear and consistent with the established scene. The target gains much more separation from darker plant masses, stronger colored accents, and firmer contact shadows; R1's small ecology mostly dissolves into the pale terrain.
- **Materials — 1.5/3.** The new pieces retain the compatible matte low-poly language, and the blue crystal reads when found in the overhead image. At normal overview and portrait scale, however, berries, flowers, foliage, and stone accents lack enough saturated surface area and value contrast to carry their gameplay roles.
- **Details — 0.2/1.** R1 has more small marks than the baseline, but it does not yet build the target's layered clusters of broad leaves, fruit, flowers, grass, stones, and discovery accents.

**Largest structural change for R2:** concentrate the ecology into three or four deliberately oversized landmark groups instead of adding more scattered instances: dense berry/leaf supply thickets at both lowland route mouths, a compact flower-and-crystal crown cluster around the Mossling habitat, and a clearly spaced chain of two-to-three-stone beats down the east return. Give each group enough footprint, height, saturation, and contact shadow to read first in the native overview and portrait cameras; counts that remain sub-pixel at those views will not solve the gap.

Keep the terrain silhouette and inherited mountain material design frozen for this slice.
