# Heartwood opening — visual judge R1

## Verdict

**8.8/10 — scoped visual pass.** Production R1 materially improves the sparse baseline and captures the target's admissible structure: asymmetrical canopy framing, a broad open north/south route, and several readable layers of low dressing from the lower clearing through the distant edge. The result is already strong enough to remain usable if another visual round does not produce a clear improvement.

This score applies the target only to grouping, negative space, depth layering, and canopy framing. It does not penalize the native render for generated HUD/avatar changes, invented foliage or model geometry, terrain treatment, lighting treatment, or camera differences that `fitness.md` explicitly excludes. Source, collision, traversal, Camp payoff, phone performance, and FPS admission are separate from this visual verdict.

## Scores

- **Composition: 2.6/3.0.** The four trees form a convincing edge frame and the central walk remains generous and legible. Low props create near, middle, and far beats rather than filling the lane. Compared with the scoped target, the distribution still reads somewhat like paired sentries: reed/flower groups occupy similar left/right bands and several small rocks sit as isolated center markers. The target's strongest admissible quality is more irregular clustering around the side masses.
- **Lighting: 2.7/3.0.** The native lighting is coherent and readable, with tree and prop shadows helping establish depth without obscuring the route. The large central canopy shadow is a useful compositional anchor. Lighting redesign from the generated target is excluded; the small deduction reflects some similarly weighted shadow patches competing across the middle band rather than a target-lighting mismatch.
- **Materials: 2.7/3.0.** Canonical tree, reed, flower, mushroom, pebble, and ground materials separate cleanly at portrait scale and remain consistent with the native game. Generated surface detail is excluded. The remaining gap is primarily visual grouping: isolated canonical pieces expose repetition more readily than tighter mixed side clusters would.
- **Details: 0.8/1.0.** The clearing has enough small beats to feel authored, and the flowers, mushrooms, reeds, and pebble groups are recognizable. A few centerline pebble marks still feel placed as route dots rather than belonging to irregular ecological pockets.

## Consequential gaps

1. The low-prop layout is more bilateral and evenly spaced than the target's irregular side clustering, especially the four prominent reed/flower groups across the upper and middle thirds.
2. Several pebble groups read as isolated centerline markers. They preserve navigation, but weaken the intended contrast between dressed edges and open floor.
3. The canonical lows do not consistently reinforce the tree masses: some stand alone while the target's admissible composition usually lets small forms gather near larger edge anchors.

## One feasible repair packet

Keep the exact **20 permanent instances** (4 solid trees and 16 lows), camera, models, terrain, lighting, HUD, avatar, collision lane, and authored identities unchanged. Reposition only a small subset of the existing low props: pull two or three isolated pebble/flower records off the center axis into uneven mixed clusters near existing side reeds or tree bases; offset one of the upper reed groups in depth so the four groups no longer form paired bands; then preserve at least the accepted four-metre trunk-free lane and roughly three metres of readable floor. Capture the same native view and accept the round only if the edge masses feel less mirrored while the central walk remains immediately obvious.
