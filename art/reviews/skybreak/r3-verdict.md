## Selection across R1–R3

**Select R3 provisionally as the strongest usable version.** R1 established dramatic relief but read as a road maze around a pit. R2 made the largest structural improvement by separating masses and opening the lowland. R3 preserves that correction while improving cap asymmetry, exposed-rock warmth, buttress width, and foot grounding. R2 retains a slightly cleaner lowland silhouette in places, but its uniformly regular pads and colder extrusion-like surfaces make it weaker overall.

The authorized best-of-three choice supports moving forward with R3 as a provisional gameplay landform. It does not convert the 4.9 score into visual admission or personal owner acceptance.

## Remaining visual debt

1. **Cluster and cap hierarchy:** the overhead still shows a mostly linear linked sequence of similar-sized pads, straight necks, and broad tan circulation bands. The target's identity depends on a dense but readable grouping of truly distinct cap islands, one dominant broad mesa, smaller irregular satellites, and a slender tower separated by green lowland fingers.
2. **Geological surface quality:** sheer faces remain large repetitive triangular curtains with abrupt dark regions. The target needs varied-width flutes, interrupted ledges, inset cracks, different plane orientations, irregular rim stone, and smaller talus/rubble accumulation that follows individual gullies rather than continuous smooth aprons.
3. **Vertical habitat and finish:** caps, routes, lower slots, and feet still lack enough distinct vegetation/material treatment. Stronger cap turf and rim growth, cooler/darker lower-slot cover, narrower localized route wear, sparse stones and plants on caps, and debris/toe vegetation would make height zones legible and bring the scene closer to the target.
