## Change from R1

R2 earns its improvement almost entirely in composition. Open ground now penetrates the formation and the eye can distinguish individual land masses; the maze/road reading is substantially reduced. The west ascent and east return also read more independently than R1's continuous loop. Lighting, material richness, and geological detail are effectively unchanged, so those categories do not inherit the topology gain.

## Final R3 priorities — maximum three

1. **Make the cap footprints organic and the cluster denser.** Preserve the open lowland fingers, but break every regular pentagon/hexagon silhouette with asymmetric lobes, bites, offsets, and unequal rim widths. Vary the hierarchy more strongly: one broad dominant cap, two or three overlapping medium caps, one narrow tower, and low shelves. Pull selected masses closer so they overlap visually in the southeast view while keeping full ground channels between most of them. Remove the detached puck shape or turn it into a narrower irregular tower. This is the largest remaining composition gain.

2. **Rebuild the connections and shoulders as erosion features.** Replace the thin straight beige bridges, needle-like east descent, and central vertical curtain with a few broader grounded gullies/saddles that widen into their support surfaces. Keep the required west ascent and east return, but interrupt their visible wear and vary their width so they do not read as constructed ramps. Break sheer faces into unequal buttresses and flutes, with occasional shallow ledges and talus-softened toes; avoid one repeated vertical triangle fan around every cap.

3. **Use one bounded surface/detail pass to establish vertical habitat.** Warm and vary exposed stone by face and crevice, darken/cool the open lower slots, increase cap-turf separation, and soften near-black planar faces with graduated shade. Add restrained cap shrubs/grass, broken rim stones, and rubble/toe vegetation concentrated around each mass. These cues should describe cap, shoulder, and lowland as distinct zones without hiding the silhouette or spending the final pass on general prop density.

## Evidence limits

The images support the visual claim that R2 has separate masses and open lowland fingers. They do not prove exact relief, mesh/collider agreement, route grounding, safe landing space, fall behavior, or a complete bidirectional traverse. Those tests should remain reported separately and should not increase this visual score.
