# Rootbound buttress study — Trial A initial massing review

**Scope:** Gate A, untextured fresh massing only. This is not a final visual, material, export, collider, or runtime review.

**Reviewed evidence:** `renders/massing_{front,rear,left,right,source_three_quarter}.png`, the approved original reference, and the corrected common construction plan.

## Decision: HOLD — repair massing before palette or export

Trial A establishes a recognizable rooted-tree hierarchy more successfully than the held v1–v3 candidates: it has a flared central trunk, three distinct structural directions, and three separately readable canopy groups. It does not pass Gate A because the all-angle images expose the two exact forms the common plan prohibits. The canopy groups collapse into thin stacked discs from left/right views, while the five roots read as separate narrow planks/prongs rather than continuous thick buttresses. Palette work would hide rather than repair these massing failures.

## Three consequential gaps

1. **Canopy side volume fails.** The left/right and three-quarter renders show broad horizontal wafers with very little real depth. The high lobe's dark horizontal slot reinforces a stacked-plate reading; it is not a deep, irregular lobe with a deliberate branch window.
2. **Root base is not yet a closed buttress mass.** The root toes are thin, rectangular or blade-like prongs and appear to join the trunk as discrete limbs. Their side profile does not meet the plan's useful 0.24 m-or-greater thickness or the target's broad merged grounded root sheets.
3. **Fork growth remains too rod-like.** The major limbs largely run as straight diagonal tubes into the canopy volumes. Their fork collars and curved growth transitions need enough asymmetric volume to remain visibly continuous in rear and side views, rather than relying on front-facing overlap.

## One consolidated geometry repair

Keep the current broad silhouette and three-direction hierarchy, but rebuild the forms as joined cross-section lofts before any palette work. Derive each buttress from a shared flared lower collar, give it two or three thick flattened loops before a rounded toe, and merge/bridge those loops so the base closes from every view. Replace each canopy wafer with two or three hand-shaped, closed irregular hulls per lobe, each with 0.45–0.85 m genuine depth and a few downward/side lobes; cut the visible fork window through volume placement rather than a thin horizontal slot. Re-loft the left/right/high limbs from shared fork collars with one shallow elbow each, then re-render the same five untextured views. This is one geometry repair, not detail work or a final admission attempt.
