# Ironspine narrow rock bank — independent target review

## Decision: HOLD before measured construction planning

The image has a useful high-level direction: one grounded, opaque, layered rock mass could replace the pair of isolated buttresses and make a more coherent left arrival edge. Its broad horizontal shelves, tapered ends, and restrained gray palette are viable ideas.

The construction sheet is internally contradictory. The displayed side elevation reads about four times as long as it is high, while the declared `3.6m X × 2.0m Y` envelope requires a 1.8:1 side-elevation ratio. Its displayed end elevation is close to square, while declared `1.0m Z × 2.0m Y` requires a 0.5:1 ratio. Perspective may distort the hero view, but it cannot reconcile the explicitly labelled orthographic-like side and end panels. The labels cannot be used as metric evidence for the visibly different shape.

The reference also leans toward a long course-built masonry wall: many near-even horizontal bands and repeated block seams would be an impractical and misleading target for one small low-poly, connected game bank. A plan should instead use three or four broad irregular ledges with varied vertical breaks, continuous solid interior, closed ends, and no brick-like repeated stones.

Before any model plan or build, regenerate or repair the reference sheet with the constrained envelope visibly enforced: side silhouette at approximately 1.8:1 X:Y, end silhouette at approximately 0.5:1 Z:Y, local axes consistent across every panel, and a single connected 3.6 × 1.0 × 2.0m grounded mass. It should retain a few asymmetrical natural shelves rather than literal block courses. The reference remains a direction artifact, not recovered orthographic geometry. No mesh or bake is authorized from this held target.
