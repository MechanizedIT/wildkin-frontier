# U4F-R Rock Reference Review

Disposition: **U4FR_OWNER_REVIEW_REQUIRED**.

The independent reviewer inspected all six saved original candidate images individually, then inspected the four preserved u4f-rock-001 images individually for comparison. The review was based on pixels, not prompts or the contact sheet.

Reviewer: independent read-only subagent /root/u4fr_pixel_review; no owner approval is implied. The reviewer shortlisted at most three candidates and selected two.

## Candidate findings

### candidate-01 — Family A, chunky slab / block boulder

Status: **STYLE_REJECT_REALISTIC**.

1. **Thumbnail/game-asset read:** the squat shape is readable but looks like a broad sandstone outcrop more than a designed game prop.
2. **Planes/facets:** the large top slabs are clear, but many small painterly chips and a deep central seam break up the planned broad-face design.
3. **Bevels:** transitions exist but look irregular and naturally broken rather than intentionally chunky.
4. **Material:** matte ochre helps, yet small painted chips compete with the large form.
5. **Project fit:** partially fits the chunky matte direction; the natural broken-rock treatment keeps it below the Wildkin style bar.
6. **Silhouette:** readable, but very squat and conventional rather than distinctive.
7. **Single closed solid:** plausible as one solid, though the apparent slabs and cracks make the interpretation less certain.
8. **False forms/shadows:** no clear through-hole; the deep central seam is the main misleading split cue. No shadow defect was reported.
9. **3D input view:** top and side volume are visible.
10. **Remaining defect:** the chipped seams and painterly natural fracture language still read as geology.

### candidate-02 — Family A, chunky slab / block boulder

Status: **STYLE_REJECT_3D_INPUT**.

1. **Thumbnail/game-asset read:** the large cap gives a memorable leaning-slab silhouette, but it remains visually noisy.
2. **Planes/facets:** major planes are strong and readable.
3. **Bevels:** broad transitions are visible, though small facet breakup weakens a deliberately authored bevel read.
4. **Material:** the blue-gray palette is matte, but dense facet texture pulls attention toward natural stone.
5. **Project fit:** the massing is substantial; natural surface detail and ambiguous structure miss the simplified asset target.
6. **Silhouette:** distinctive mushroom-like cap and leaning support.
7. **Single closed solid:** not reliable from this view; the cleft beneath the cap appears to separate an upper slab from the supports.
8. **False forms/shadows:** the open undercut can read as a false hole or disconnected/cantilevered piece.
9. **3D input view:** top and side information is clear.
10. **Remaining defect:** the cleft and fine surface breakup make the closed-solid interpretation too risky.

### candidate-03 — Family B, rounded faceted boulder

Status: **STYLE_REJECT_GENERIC**.

1. **Thumbnail/game-asset read:** clearly a low-poly boulder, but an undifferentiated rounded block.
2. **Planes/facets:** large front and top planes read cleanly; the center seam pattern is generic.
3. **Bevels:** shallow bevels are visible but not particularly chunky.
4. **Material:** the matte color treatment is appropriately simple and form remains clear.
5. **Project fit:** matte/faceted in broad terms, but the shape is too primitive to carry the requested authored character.
6. **Silhouette:** nearly symmetrical lump with little reuse identity.
7. **Single closed solid:** the clearest closed-solid read in the set.
8. **False forms/shadows:** no obvious false hole, fused neighbor, or misleading shadow.
9. **3D input view:** top and side information is clear.
10. **Remaining defect:** generic primitive-like silhouette and seam pattern.

### candidate-04 — Family B, rounded faceted boulder

Status: **STYLE_SHORTLIST**.

1. **Thumbnail/game-asset read:** the strongest game-asset read in the set; broad stepped terraces remain legible at thumbnail size.
2. **Planes/facets:** large purposeful horizontal and vertical planes define the form.
3. **Bevels:** chunky bevel transitions remain readable.
4. **Material:** broad matte ochre and dusty-rose value blocks carry the form; small painterly mottling remains.
5. **Project fit:** strong match for substantial, matte, faceted game-art forms.
6. **Silhouette:** distinctive offset high rear cap above the lower front mass.
7. **Single closed solid:** plausible as one fused, closed solid despite the terrace steps.
8. **False forms/shadows:** a few seams suggest assembled chunks, but no deceptive hole is apparent.
9. **3D input view:** top and multiple sides are visible.
10. **Remaining defect:** small mottling and front/right seams could still read as natural assembled chunks.

### candidate-05 — Family C, asymmetrical alien wedge / fracture boulder

Status: **STYLE_REJECT_REALISTIC**.

1. **Thumbnail/game-asset read:** substantial stepped massing, but the mineral streaks make it read like illustrated natural ore.
2. **Planes/facets:** the large stepped faces are clear; pervasive small facets compete with them.
3. **Bevels:** the main shoulders are rounded, but small broken edges dominate too much of the surface.
4. **Material:** matte blue-gray is interrupted by teal streaks and frequent surface variation.
5. **Project fit:** the mass is useful, but the ore/lichen-like material pattern and natural detail are too prominent.
6. **Silhouette:** the raised rear and low front create a usable wedge profile.
7. **Single closed solid:** plausible, though dark notches and narrow seams reduce confidence.
8. **False forms/shadows:** no through-hole is visible; dark notches and seams are the main ambiguity.
9. **3D input view:** rear-to-front terraces show the top and sides.
10. **Remaining defect:** natural mineral/lichen cues and pervasive small faceting compete with the designed form.

### candidate-06 — Family C, asymmetrical alien wedge / fracture boulder

Status: **STYLE_SHORTLIST**.

1. **Thumbnail/game-asset read:** substantial stylized game formation with a readable rising-ramp profile.
2. **Planes/facets:** dominant broad planes are easy to distinguish.
3. **Bevels:** thick transitions read at thumbnail size.
4. **Material:** muted lavender-gray and sage-slate regions are matte and simplified, though some small paint texture remains.
5. **Project fit:** good match for the project's substantial matte/faceted visual language.
6. **Silhouette:** the rising ramp and uneven front create a distinct profile.
7. **Single closed solid:** plausible as one fused, closed solid from this view.
8. **False forms/shadows:** the diagonal center-front join and color regions can read as component boundaries, but do not form an apparent open hole.
9. **3D input view:** left, right, and top faces are visible.
10. **Remaining defect:** the center-front join and paint texture could be simplified further.

## Comparison against u4f-rock-001

The old gray candidates carry higher-frequency granular/chipped detail, thin fracture networks, and natural erosion/cavity cues. Their studio presentation remains close to realistic geology. The new set uses broader painted color fields, clearer large planes, and thicker edge treatment. Candidates 04 and 06 visibly move into the intended matte/faceted game-asset language. Candidates 01, 02, and 05 retain too much natural-geology detail; candidate 03 simplifies too far into a generic primitive. This comparison is about the actual saved images, not stronger prompt wording.
