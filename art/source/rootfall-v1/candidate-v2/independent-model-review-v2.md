## Why the held issues close

1. **The body now has a directional biological silhouette.** Front and quarter CLOSED show a higher, fuller left buttress descending into a lower, thinner right branch system. Uneven upper contours and larger varying facets replace the long level cylinder and repeated color belts. The body and end roots read as one fallen organism. Teal areas now belong to the bark surfaces instead of looking like floating flat stickers. The faceted brown/teal treatment fits the Explorer family sufficiently for this gate, although the reference is richer and less geometrically regular.

2. **The working sites now read as shallow chips.** `notch-detail.png` shows diagonal broken skin fragments in a jagged exposed sapwood shape, with substantial intact wood above/below and no circumferential pale ring. The straight rectangular sill and three upright plugs from V1 are gone. At player scale, the two distinct tan slashes remain visible on the near face. They are small starter cuts, consistent with the contract's roughly0.3m extent, rather than an already severed trunk. Live target labels, tool reach and depletion readability are still native obligations.

3. **The lashings visibly bind the paired shafts.** `brace-contact.png` shows three continuous-looking turns around both upper timbers below their bearing, with a short return. Their silhouette and placement now explain the connection; the V1 arcs on bark above the joint are gone. Both feet are planted in the neutral ground and upper timbers meet the retained wood. Four timbers and two lashings remain recognizable in the OPEN views.

## Remaining finish limits and native obligations

The broad teal polygon blocks on the left still have conspicuous rectangular edges; the plain cut end caps and sparse exterior growth are less rich than the target. These are later art polish, not unresolved V1 structural blockers. If refined, use bounded irregular color boundaries or sparse growth around the outer banks without adding surface noise or changing the fixed geometry contract.

Native review must still establish ground contact on the retained ridge, plausible support of the main body and every timber foot, meaningful cut/brace targeting, the cut-but-unbraced visible blocker, actual closed/open collision, approach and reciprocal return clearance, save/reload, and camera/HUD readability. An outer stiff root finger projecting over a slope can be plausible; no rule requires every tip vertex to touch terrain. This review does not decide new jump mechanics or alter the cut-height contract.
