# Rootfall V4 — independent native visual review

**Verdict: HOLD — 7.1/10 on the DreamLoop visual rubric.** V4 materially improves the two former fitting-like cut points: the work sites are now visible at the live 844x390 camera, look like warm exposed wood rather than metal inserts, and the open passage/brace state remains clear. It still falls short of native admission because CLOSED retains a dark, nearly level underbody that reads as an elevated arch supported by root legs. The bright kerfs also remain wider and more belt-like in the native frame than the target's shallow, irregular front chips. The visible asset therefore does not yet fully match the grounded continuous obstacle required by the locked V3 target.

This is a review of the supplied native captures and fresh exported-model images. The surrounding first-terrain blockout is deliberately not scored; it is under separate revision. No browser, Blender, runtime, model, collider or source edit was performed.

## What V4 closes

- The two front work locations are now easy to find in `native-closed.png` and do not look like the former small square fittings. `notch-detail.png` confirms a tapered exposed-sapwood surface with an irregular adjacent bark edge.
- `native-open.png` retains a clear central passage and visible destination beyond it. `native-return-exit.png` makes the reciprocal opening legible, while the native receipt records actual passage traversal to z −33.04 and the supported return at z −31.5.
- The two brace pairs remain outside the lane and visibly carry the surviving ends. The **Brace passage** contextual button and resource cost are legible even with the current landscape HUD and new Jump control present.

## Remaining native blockers

1. **CLOSED still appears pass-under-able.** The lower trunk forms a very dark, straight horizontal band between large planted root legs. Although the diagnostic confirms collision, its picture reads more like an elevated arch/bridge than the V3 target's continuous root meeting the travel surface. Do not solve this with an invisible blocker. Make the central lower body visibly descend/overlap into the route-floor silhouette, with irregular warm bark/root mass that closes the apparent under-gap while retaining the overall width and cut planes.

2. **The kerfs are too band-like at the game camera.** Their light tan faces are now readable, but each presents as a tall, high-contrast vertical stripe across the lower trunk. Reduce the continuous vertical extent/brightness visible from the approach and let asymmetrical dark bark lips cover more of its upper and lower edges. Preserve a small, unmistakable pale bite and intact bark around the core; do not revert to invisible details, complete rings, or metallic panels.

3. **First-cut progression remains visually weak.** `native-first-cut.png` shows the status toast and full remaining trunk, but the changed west side is difficult to identify from its ordinary approach. Retain the deliberate still-blocked state, but add a clear small broken/chipped result at the completed side—visible before the second cut—so the physical and textual states agree.

## Fresh model-evidence notes

The close neutral image supports that V4 is a real curved/tapered sapwood revision rather than the V2 square-panel material. The fresh OPEN view also supports that the same retained left/right forms become separated and braced; it is not a second generated tree. Those observations do **not** waive the native target mismatch: V3 calls for a continuous grounded CLOSED center and shallow front notches, while the actual V4 native silhouette still exposes an arch-like underbody and visually broad bands. The manifest's valid assets, fixed planes and reserved lane are useful structural receipts, not visual acceptance.
