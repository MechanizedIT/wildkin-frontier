# Rootbound Wildwood whole-map v1 — independent concept review

## Status: useful art-direction reference; HOLD as a literal spatial or technical target

Reviewed `whole-map-v1.png`, SHA-256 `888D5F2C8E265D7AFFE3BA9DA059C283696DAEB34B3A07ABB8EAE63FA59EB742`.

### What to retain

The illustration has high presentation quality and gives the five zones distinct values at a glance: an open bright Meadow, warm root-led Galleries and Crown, a cool violet Lantern focal pocket, and a restrained grey mineral Verge. The pale route-color hierarchy and Lantern contrast offer useful high-level reference for the later portrait family. Retain those relationships, the warm/cool material separation, and the decision to make the Meadow visibly quieter than the denser inner rooms.

### Why it cannot become a literal map

It is substantially denser, taller, and more enclosed than the available Rootbound kit and portrait play space. The heavy tree blanket, steep rock-wall chains, large root crossings, and lush undergrowth create repeated occlusion and imply impassable/unsupported relief. The path network does not clearly express the reviewed west return or a readable central safe lane. Several composed root forms cross likely travel space, while the Verge looks like a rocky cliff corridor rather than a low optional mineral edge. These are art-direction exaggerations, not evidence for terrain, asset placement, collision, ecology, or a 3–5 minute circuit.

### Required interpretation for follow-up images

Use it as a **palette, density-contrast, and zone-sequence mood reference only**. Future portrait directions should:

1. preserve the Meadow’s open floor and a visible eastward transition cue;
2. place root architecture on room edges, with no route-spanning arches or root crossings;
3. keep Lantern’s restrained localized contrast without magical glow or a dark pit;
4. reduce Verge to low broken shards at an escape-lane edge; and
5. replace the tree-wall perimeter with sparse, camera-projected anchor masses and deliberate sightline gaps.

No coordinates, terrain heights, route geometry, kit equivalence, or runtime placement should be copied from this image. A later map revision may reuse its sequence only after the actual planning map, native poses, support, and clearance checks determine what fits.
