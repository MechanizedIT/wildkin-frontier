## Modeling provisions

1. **Four anatomical limb chains only.** The near shoulder and haunch connect broadly to the torso. Far upper-limb insertions are partly occluded by the belly and near limbs; author their homologous connections deliberately. Preserve a small separation around each lower leg and paw. Do not convert faceted shoulder patches into a second floating shoulder shell or extra joint. Toe divisions belong to a single broad paw, not detached digits.
2. **Treat eyes and cheek fins as attached paired anatomy.** The far eye is seen as a narrow dark protruding sliver at the head silhouette; its socket and pale rim are not fully specified. Place the far eye symmetrically in the skull rather than reconstructing that sliver as a detached bead. Only the near cheek fin's full shape is clear: give both plates a broad flush root and modest thickness, with no deep undercut that severs them from the jaw/cheek. A front view of the eventual mesh must verify alignment.
3. **One connected dorsal crest.** Join all three peaks through a continuous opaque root strip on the spine; do not recreate the prototype's floating tiles. The peaks are fins, not three independently rooted horns. Keep their base stable under back deformation.
4. **Maintain a continuous substantial tail.** The pictured taper and terminal paddle are readable, but the fluke's far thickness and exact rear silhouette are not specified. Keep a continuous tail volume and a broad supported paddle transition; avoid a narrow rigid rod, sharp pinched hinge or separate floating fan. Tail posing must clear both hind legs.
5. **Model a neutral head/spine rather than freezing perspective.** Preserve forward head alignment, level pelvis and ordinary shoulder/hip rotation space. Hidden ventral/neck surfaces must be closed. Judge fresh front, rear and side mesh views before rigging admission; do not infer those surfaces are proven by this one image.
